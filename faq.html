Planning a Project With Carve Creation

Frequently Asked
Questions

Carve Creation manages design development, custom manufacturing, and delivery across five core capabilities—contract furniture, branding and wayfinding, architectural lighting, Carve Flex™ deployable spaces, and public art and sculpture.

The questions below cover how we work, what information is needed to begin, how pricing and prototypes are handled, and what to expect through production, quality control, delivery, and installation.

About Carve Creation

Carve Creation translates design intent into manufactured products and environments for the built environment.

Our five core capabilities are:

  • Contract Furniture
  • Branding & Wayfinding
  • Architectural Lighting
  • Carve Flex™ Deployable Spaces
  • Public Art & Sculpture

We coordinate technical development, materials, prototypes, manufacturing, milestone inspections, packaging, and delivery through one managed process.

Our primary strength is manufacturing. We typically work from concepts developed by architects, interior designers, lighting designers, branding consultants, artists, and client teams.

When a design is incomplete, we can provide the development needed to make it manufacturable. Broader original design services can also be coordinated when required, but they are defined separately from our core manufacturing scope.

We work with developers, architects, interior designers, artists, brands, hospitality groups, workplace teams, institutions, contractors, and other organizations responsible for creating physical environments.

Carve Creation is best suited to projects involving one or more of the following:

  • Custom or technically complex products
  • Multiple materials or manufacturing processes
  • Coordinated product families
  • Prototype development followed by production
  • Repeated quantities or multiple locations
  • Integrated furniture, lighting, branding, or technology
  • Specialized finishes or fabrication methods
  • Defined quality-control and documentation requirements

Yes. A project may begin with one product family or combine furniture, branding, lighting, deployable spaces, and public art within a broader environment.

Using one coordination and manufacturing framework helps maintain a consistent design language across different product categories.

Design and Technical Development

Yes. This is our primary role. We review the design intent and translate it into materials, assemblies, details, prototypes, and production-ready information.

Yes. We can help resolve dimensions, materials, construction, finishes, hardware, connections, and other information needed for pricing and manufacturing.

The extent of design development depends on the information provided and the complexity of the project.

Our objective is to protect the essential design intent while making the product practical to manufacture, transport, install, maintain, and reproduce.

If a technical, cost, schedule, or material issue requires a change, we present the options for review before proceeding.

Design for Manufacturing and Assembly, or DfMA, is an approach that considers how a product will be fabricated, assembled, transported, installed, maintained, and potentially repeated while it is being developed.

This helps identify unnecessary complexity, reduce production risks, and improve consistency without losing the intended aesthetic.

Engineering can be coordinated when required, but responsibility depends on the product, project location, and applicable regulations.

The need for calculations, stamped documents, testing, or review by a locally licensed professional should be identified at the beginning of the project.

Yes. We can evaluate materials and finishes in relation to the intended appearance, application, durability, manufacturing process, maintenance, budget, and schedule.

Final selections are documented and submitted for approval before production.

Getting Started and Pricing

We can usually begin with the information already available, such as:

  • Drawings, sketches, or renderings
  • Reference images
  • Preliminary dimensions
  • Estimated quantities
  • Material and finish preferences
  • Project location
  • Target budget
  • Required schedule
  • Performance or certification requirements

Complete construction documents are not required for an initial conversation. After reviewing the available material, we will identify what additional information is needed.

Often, yes. A preliminary estimate may be possible when the approximate dimensions, quantities, materials, location, and expected level of quality are known.

The estimate may need to be refined as the design and technical requirements are developed.

Minimum quantities depend on the capability, materials, tooling, and level of customization.

A prototype or individual specialized piece may be feasible, but our manufacturing platform generally provides greater value for coordinated product families, repeated quantities, or technically demanding work.

Yes. Sharing the target budget early allows us to evaluate scale, materials, construction, finishes, quantities, and other decisions in relation to the project’s priorities.

A target budget does not guarantee that every design can be delivered within that amount, but it helps prevent development around an unsuitable solution.

Pricing may be affected by:

  • Design completeness
  • Dimensions and quantities
  • Materials and finishes
  • Manufacturing complexity
  • Samples and prototypes
  • Engineering or testing requirements
  • Packaging and freight
  • Installation or site services
  • Project schedule
  • Delivery location

A detailed quotation is prepared once the scope and responsibilities are sufficiently defined.

The schedule depends on the product type, design completeness, quantities, materials, samples, approvals, testing, manufacturing complexity, and delivery location.

We establish the expected milestones and overall schedule after reviewing the project information. Client review and approval time should be considered separately from manufacturing time.

Samples, Prototypes, and Approvals

Yes. Appropriate material and finish samples can be submitted for review before production.

The sample process depends on the materials, finishes, quantities, and degree of customization.

Yes. A project may include a detail mockup, partial prototype, representative product, or complete working prototype.

The appropriate level of prototyping is determined by the product’s complexity, quantity, risk, and approval requirements.

No. A prototype may not be necessary for a straightforward product or small quantity. It becomes more valuable when a project involves complex construction, specialized finishes, integrated systems, repeated production, or a significant quantity.

Approvals may include:

  • Dimensions and drawings
  • Materials and finishes
  • Colors and textures
  • Hardware and components
  • Construction details
  • Lighting or electrical elements
  • Samples, mockups, or prototypes
  • Packaging and identification requirements

The applicable approval milestones are defined for each project.

Changes may still be possible, but they can affect cost, schedule, completed work, purchased materials, tooling, and production planning.

Any proposed change should be documented and approved before revised work proceeds.

Manufacturing and Quality Assurance

Production is assigned through our specialized international manufacturing network according to the required materials, processes, quality standards, quantities, schedule, and project location.

Carve Creation remains responsible for coordinating development, approvals, manufacturing records, milestone inspections, and delivery.

Quality is managed through defined approval and inspection milestones rather than relying only on a final inspection.

Depending on the project, this may include:

  • Material and finish approvals
  • Shop-drawing review
  • Prototype or mockup approval
  • First-article inspection
  • In-process inspections
  • Assembly and functional checks
  • Final visual and dimensional inspection
  • Packaging documentation

Project decisions, approvals, milestone records, schedules, and production documentation can be tracked through the Carve Creation project portal.

Progress photographs and other required records are provided at the agreed project milestones.

Yes. Approved drawings, materials, finish references, samples, tooling, and inspection criteria establish the manufacturing standard for repeated production.

Natural materials and handcrafted finishes may still include reasonable variation, which should be defined during the approval process.

Yes. Approved products or product families can be adapted into a controlled manufacturing standard for phased or multi-location deployment.

Quantities, site-specific variations, labeling, packaging, and delivery requirements can be coordinated for each location.

Delivery and Installation

Yes. Packaging and freight can be coordinated according to the product, dimensions, destination, site access, and required delivery method.

International delivery may also be evaluated depending on the project.

Packaging is developed according to the size, weight, fragility, finish, transportation method, and site-handling requirements.

Depending on the project, this may include custom crates, internal supports, protective wrapping, component identification, and unpacking instructions.

Installation, site attendance, assembly supervision, and installer coordination can be included when appropriate.

The exact responsibility for unloading, local labor, equipment, electrical connections, foundations, site preparation, and final installation must be defined in the project scope.

Yes. We can coordinate product information, packaging, component identification, and installer handoff requirements with the client’s local team.

The shipment should be inspected upon arrival, and any visible damage should be documented immediately before or during unpacking.

The appropriate response depends on the shipping terms, insurance, product condition, and responsibility for transportation and handling.

Spare parts, replacement components, or attic stock can be included when appropriate.

We recommend identifying critical components early, especially for repeated programs, installed systems, custom lighting, and products that may require future maintenance.
