Overview

Light as a Designed, Fabricated Element.

Lighting is one of the most visible ways a project's design intent is experienced, and one of the easiest places for that intent to be lost between drawing and installation. Carve Creation manufactures decorative and architectural fixtures as a coordinated part of the built environment package—aligned with the materials, finishes, and details defined elsewhere in the project.

A single project may include dozens of fixture types across pendant, sconce, cove, linear, and accent conditions. Carve Creation manages these fixture families against common standards for material, finish, and light quality, while preserving the unique requirements of feature and custom pieces.

Two Lighting Categories

Decorative Lighting

Decorative fixtures are experienced as design objects in their own right—selected for form, finish, and character as much as illumination. These pieces often carry the most visible design intent in a space and require the closest coordination on material, finish, and scale.

  • Pendants and chandeliers
  • Wall sconces
  • Table and floor fixtures
  • Feature and statement fixtures
  • Custom and artist-designed lighting

Architectural Lighting

Architectural fixtures are built into the space itself—shaping how surfaces, materials, and volumes read after dark. These fixtures are typically coordinated closely with ceiling, wall, and millwork details rather than selected as standalone objects.

  • Cove and linear lighting
  • Recessed and accent lighting
  • Wall-wash and grazing fixtures
  • Integrated millwork and casework lighting
  • Exterior and façade lighting
Materials and Coordination

Fixtures are manufactured to align with the finishes, materials, and mounting conditions defined elsewhere in the project—coordinated color temperature, dimming, and control requirements where specified, and manufactured to the approved design intent rather than a generic catalog equivalent.

Final feasibility depends on the approved design, dimensions, site conditions, performance requirements, and responsibility matrix.

How the Package Is Managed
1

Design and Specification Review

We review fixture drawings, design intent renderings, cut sheets, finish standards, photometric requirements, quantities, and delivery requirements.

2

Clarification and Production Definition

Inconsistencies between drawings, finish specifications, quantities, and electrical requirements are identified for resolution. Production proceeds only from an approved information set.

3

Samples and Mockups

Finish samples, material samples, or illuminated prototypes may be required to confirm color temperature, light quality, and finish before full production.

4

Fabrication Control

Each approved fixture family is translated into a coordinated production package. Repeat fixtures are managed against common standards while feature and custom pieces retain their project-specific requirements.

5

Packaging and Delivery Coordination

Fixtures are packaged to protect finishes and components in transit, and delivery is coordinated with the agreed installation and receiving plan.

Required Project Information
  • Fixture drawings, cut sheets, or design intent package
  • Finish and material specifications
  • Quantities
  • Voltage, wattage, and control or dimming requirements
  • Mounting conditions
  • Project location
  • Required delivery date
  • Applicable code, certification, or testing requirements

Unless expressly included in our proposal, Carve Creation does not perform field verification, site measurement, permitting, engineering, electrical connection, or installation. The client, designer, contractor, and electrician remain responsible for confirming site conditions, code compliance, circuiting, final locations, and installation requirements before production.
