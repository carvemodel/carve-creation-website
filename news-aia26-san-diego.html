Carve Creation at AIA26 in San Diego

Carve Creation attended the AIA Conference on Architecture & Design 2026, held June 10–13 at the San Diego Convention Center.

By Carve Creation · June 2026

Carve Creation attended the AIA Conference on Architecture & Design 2026, held June 10–13 at the San Diego Convention Center.

The event brought together architects, designers, builders, manufacturers, and technology companies to exchange ideas about the changing practice of architecture.

Design is becoming more abundant

Across the conference, conversations addressed subjects including AI, new building technologies, sustainability, adaptive reuse, and the future of architectural practice.

For Carve, one question was especially relevant: as digital tools make it easier to generate, visualize, and revise design options, how do teams determine what should move forward, and what should actually get built?

More design possibilities do not automatically make decisions clearer. Architects still need to communicate scale, proportion, context, material relationships, and spatial intent to clients, consultants, approval agencies, investors, and the public.

From approved design to manufactured reality

Renderings, BIM models, and AI-generated images can each communicate important parts of a project. Precision manufacturing provides something different: a real object, built from the actual specified materials, that performs under the same conditions the finished space will experience.

Materials read differently at full scale than they do on screen. Finishes behave differently under real light. Connections, tolerances, and hardware either hold up or they do not. A sample or first-article piece lets a design team confirm those qualities before committing to full production—around the same table, looking at the same object.

That does not make manufacturing a replacement for digital design. It makes it a complementary form of evidence—one that can support evaluation, approval, presentation, and commitment before a project reaches the field.

What AIA26 reinforced for Carve Creation

The conference reinforced the direction behind Carve Creation’s approach: defining what a design is meant to achieve—visually, functionally, and over its service life—before determining materials, construction methods, and manufacturing standards.

As design production accelerates, custom furniture, branding and wayfinding, architectural lighting, deployable spaces, and public art all benefit from the same discipline: samples, prototypes, and milestone inspections that verify a design in real materials before it reaches full production. Not every detail needs a prototype, but the decisions that matter most benefit from something a project team can examine together in real space.

We left San Diego encouraged by the conversations taking place across the profession, and by the continuing importance of coordinated physical manufacturing within increasingly digital design and delivery workflows. Learn more about how we manage that process, or explore our capabilities.
